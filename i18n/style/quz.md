- Write commands and action buttons as direct, singular imperatives, usually ending in -y or -chiy. Omit the subject and any polite pronoun. (Uraykachiy; Sayachiy)
- Phrase menu items as concise actions when they trigger an action; keep the same imperative form used on buttons. (Khiputa uraykachiy)
- Make checkbox labels short and state-like or action-oriented. Do not add a full sentence unless the choice needs clarification. (Windowswan qallariy)
- Address the user directly in a neutral, respectful register. Quechua imperatives do not require a formal second-person pronoun; avoid importing Spanish usted. (Akllay; Kay khiputa waqaychay)
- Phrase confirmation prompts as complete questions. Use the direct second-person form for asking what the user wants. (¿Kay khiputa waqaychayta munankichu?)
- Use concise noun phrases for labels and column titles. Quechua has no articles; omit them, and avoid adding prepositions unless the relationship would otherwise be unclear. (Suti; Khipupa sutin)
- Prefer telegraphic wording for column headings and compact UI labels; use a full phrase only when needed to make the meaning unambiguous. (Suti; Uraykachiy pachakuna)
- Render ongoing activity with an ongoing aspect form. Keep progress wording short and avoid adding an English-style subject pronoun. (Qhawachkan…; {n} khipukuna uraykachichkanku)
- Keep error messages grammatical even when they appear as fragments. Put the cause or detail after a colon, and preserve the supplied error placeholder exactly. (Kichariyta mana atirqanchu: {error})
- Use concise past-tense statements for event log entries. Include an actor only when it is needed to distinguish who performed the action. (Uraykachiy tukurqan.)
- Use sentence case, not title case: capitalize the first word and proper names, but not every word in a heading. Write month and weekday names in lowercase. (Khiputa uraykachiy; lunes, enero)
- Follow standard Latin-script punctuation. Use opening and closing question marks for questions; use an ellipsis for ongoing activity only where it helps convey progress. (¿Khiputa waqaychayta munankichu?; Qhawachkan…)
- Use a single space between words and no spaces before grammatical suffixes. Keep placeholders attached to surrounding text only where the source syntax requires it. (khipukuna; {n} khipukuna)
- Use « » for quotation marks in prose where supported. Do not put ordinary UI labels in quotation marks. («Khiputa uraykachiy» nispa akllay.)
- Let the verb ending carry the implied subject and number. Do not add a pronoun merely to make the subject explicit. (Tukurqan.; Khipukuna uraykachichkanku.)
- Keep the usual Quechua order: object and other complements before the verb. Put descriptive modifiers before the noun they modify. (Khiputa uraykachiy; musuq khipu)
- Build compounds from familiar Quechua words where that makes a term clear and compact. Keep established technical loans and product names rather than inventing obscure replacements. (khipu waqaychana; Windows)
- Use the same term for the same feature throughout the app, including in menus, notifications, and logs. (Uraykachiy; Uraykachiy tukurqan.)
- Do not translate a variable, product name, file extension, or protocol name unless the product glossary explicitly provides a localized form. ({n}; Windows)
- Keep status messages factual and brief. Avoid adding a subject or explanatory wording that is not present in the source. (Sayachisqa; Tukurqan)
- Preserve the source message’s distinction between stopping, pausing, and continuing. Do not use one action label for all three. (Samachiy; Sayachiy)
