- Use the verb in the infinitive for commands, button labels and menu actions. Keep labels short and omit terminal punctuation. (Eroflueden; Späicheren)
- Phrase check box labels as short actions or conditions, not as questions. Omit the full stop. (Beim Start automatesch eroflueden)
- Address the user formally as „Dir“; capitalize the pronoun and related forms such as „Är“. Keep this register consistent. (Wielt Ären Download-Dossier aus.)
- Use a direct, complete question for confirmations, with formal address where the user is addressed. (Wëllt Dir dësen Download wierklech läschen?)
- Use concise noun phrases for labels and column headings. Usually omit articles and prepositions unless needed for clarity. (Download-Dossier; Dateinumm)
- Write progress messages as brief, natural status phrases. Use count-aware singular and plural forms for variable quantities. (Gëtt iwwerpréift…; 1 Download gëtt erofgelueden)
- Keep error messages clear and actionable. A fragment is appropriate when the error detail follows a colon. (Konnt net opgemaach ginn: {error})
- Use compact past-tense phrasing for event-log entries; include a full sentence when the context needs one. (Download gestart; D'Datei gouf erofgelueden.)
- Use sentence case, not English-style title case. Luxembourgish nouns are capitalized; days of the week and months are capitalized as nouns. (Download starten; Méindeg, 12. Januar)
- Follow standard punctuation spacing: no space before commas, full stops, question marks or colons; use one space after them. Use an ellipsis for an ongoing state. (Wëllt Dir fortfueren?; Feeler: Verbindung ënnerbrach)
- Use Luxembourgish quotation marks („…“) for quoted text. Do not add quotation marks around ordinary interface labels. (De Status „Fäerdeg“ gouf aktualiséiert.)
- In a status fragment with an implied subject, do not add a user-directed verb form. Prefer a neutral state or passive construction. (Pauséiert; D'Datei gëtt erofgelueden.)
- Keep normal Luxembourgish word order: the finite verb is generally second in a main clause, while separable verb parts go to the end. (D'Datei gëtt elo erofgelueden.; Wann den Download fäerdeg ass…)
- Use established technical loanwords where they are familiar, but prefer a clear Luxembourgish verb when one exists. Do not mix English into a sentence without need. (de Download; eroflueden)
- Preserve placeholders exactly, including their braces, and place them where Luxembourgish grammar requires. (Konnt {fileName} net opgemaach ginn: {error})
- Use the same term consistently for the same feature throughout the app; distinguish an action from its resulting item or state. (eroflueden (action); Download (item or process))
- Avoid unnecessary articles in short status and menu fragments, but use them in full sentences when natural. (Download fäerdeg; Den Download ass fäerdeg.)
- Use a hyphen in a compound only when it improves readability or separates a technical name; otherwise write ordinary compounds together. (Download-Dossier; Dateinumm)
