- Commands, menu actions, and command buttons usually use the Lithuanian infinitive. Use the formal plural imperative in instructions addressed to the user. (Atsisiųsti; Pristabdyti)
- Phrase check boxes as concise options, usually with an infinitive action or a short noun phrase. Keep the label clear about what enabling the box does. (Paleisti atsisiuntimą automatiškai; Rodyti pranešimus)
- Address the user politely and consistently. Use the formal plural imperative for instructions; avoid informal singular forms and unnecessary pronouns. (Įveskite failo adresą.; Pasirinkite atsisiuntimo aplanką.)
- Phrase confirmation prompts as direct questions, commonly beginning with „Ar tikrai…?“ State the consequence when it is useful. (Ar tikrai norite pašalinti šį atsisiuntimą?)
- Use concise noun phrases for labels and column titles. Lithuanian has no articles; omit prepositions unless they clarify the relationship. (Failo vardas; Atsisiuntimo aplankas)
- Write progress and transient status text impersonally, using a natural Lithuanian verb form. Preserve placeholders and make the surrounding wording agree with their meaning. (Tikrinama…; Atsisiunčiama {n} failų)
- Translate error fragments so they can be followed naturally by the inserted error text. Do not add punctuation that would duplicate punctuation supplied by the application. (Nepavyko atidaryti: {error})
- Write event-log entries as concise, complete statements, normally in the past tense. Use a period when the entry is a sentence. (Atsisiuntimas pradėtas.; Failas įrašytas.)
- Use sentence case for titles, headings, labels, and menu items; capitalize only the first word and proper names. (Atsisiuntimo parametrai; Pranešimų sritis)
- Write Lithuanian month and weekday names in lowercase, except at the beginning of a sentence. (pirmadienis; sausis)
- Use Lithuanian punctuation. Do not put a space before a comma, period, colon, or question mark; put one space after it. Use the single ellipsis character for an ongoing action. (Tikrinama…; Nepavyko atidaryti: {error})
- Use Lithuanian quotation marks („…“). Use a nonbreaking space between a number and a unit or percent sign where the layout allows. (Failas „ataskaita.zip“; 50 %)
- Make the verb agree with its expressed subject. For subjectless status text, use an impersonal form rather than forcing agreement with an implied English subject. (Atsisiuntimas baigtas.; Atsisiunčiama…)
- Prefer natural Lithuanian word order: put descriptive words next to the words they modify, and avoid copying English order when it sounds unnatural. (Didelis failas; Automatiškai paleisti atsisiuntimą)
- Use established Lithuanian terms instead of unnecessary English loanwords. Retain product names, file extensions, and standard technical abbreviations when appropriate. (atsisiuntimas (not „downloadas“); URL)
- Keep terminology and grammatical form consistent across the interface, especially for the same action in a menu, button, and status message. (Pristabdyti; Atsisiuntimas pristabdytas)
- Use short labels where space is limited, but do not omit information needed to distinguish similar actions or settings. (Greitis; Atsisiuntimo greitis)
- Keep placeholders unchanged. Place them where Lithuanian grammar requires, and do not add endings inside a placeholder. (Nepavyko atidaryti: {error}; Atsisiunčiama {n} failų)
- Use the same Lithuanian term for a feature throughout the interface; choose a different verb only when the meaning differs, such as verifying versus trying a feature. (Tikrinti failą; Išbandyti funkciją)
