- Use the polite second-person plural imperative for instructions addressed to the user. Avoid informal singular forms. (Selectați fișierul.; Introduceți adresa URL.)
- Keep button labels short. Prefer established action nouns or infinitive forms for common actions; use a clear imperative when the button is phrased as an instruction. (Salvare; Anulare)
- Use the same concise action-label style in menus. Keep menu items parallel when they are part of the same group. (Pauză; Reluare)
- Phrase check box labels as short statements or noun phrases, not as questions. Make clear what selecting the box does. (Pornire automată la pornirea Windows; Afișați notificările)
- Address users with the polite plural forms „dvs.” or implied „dumneavoastră”; usually omit the pronoun when the verb form is clear. Maintain a neutral, helpful register. (Selectați folderul în care doriți să salvați fișierele.; Puteți relua descărcarea oricând.)
- Write confirmation prompts as complete, direct questions. Name the action and, where useful, its consequence. (Doriți să opriți descărcarea?; Sigur doriți să ștergeți fișierul?)
- Keep labels and column headings concise, usually as nouns without an article. Use prepositions where needed for natural Romanian; do not force telegraphic wording that obscures meaning. (Nume; Dimensiune)
- Use natural Romanian for progress and status messages. For an action in progress, prefer an impersonal construction with „se”; use an ellipsis only when the message is genuinely ongoing. (Se verifică…; Descărcări în curs: {n})
- Use plural-aware resources for counted items so the noun agrees with the number. Avoid templates that produce forms such as „1 descărcări”. (1 fișier se descarcă; {n} fișiere se descarcă)
- Turn English error fragments into clear Romanian messages. Use a complete construction rather than copying English word order. (Nu s-a putut deschide: {error}.; Fișierul nu a putut fi salvat.)
- Write event log entries as concise, informative statements. Include the relevant item or action when available, and use consistent tense within the log. (Descărcarea fișierului s-a finalizat.; Conexiunea a fost întreruptă.)
- Use sentence case for titles, headings, buttons, and menu items: capitalize the first word and proper names, not every word. (Setări de descărcare; Opțiuni de alimentare)
- Write Romanian month and weekday names with lowercase initial letters, except at the beginning of a sentence. (15 martie; luni)
- Follow Romanian punctuation conventions: do not put a space before punctuation; put one space after it. Use a comma for Romanian decimal fractions. (Descărcare finalizată.; Viteză: 2,5 MB/s)
- Use Romanian quotation marks when quoting text: „…” . Do not add spaces inside quotation marks. (Mesajul „Fișierul există deja” a fost afișat.; Selectați „Reluare”.)
- Romanian can omit the subject when it is clear. Ensure verbs and adjectives still agree with the subject; avoid gendered fragments when the subject is unknown. (Descărcarea s-a finalizat.; Fișierele sunt pregătite.)
- Use natural Romanian word order and place modifiers where they read clearly. Reorder placeholders as needed instead of preserving English syntax. (Se descarcă fișierul {name}.; Au fost găsite {n} rezultate.)
- Prefer established Romanian terms over English borrowings or literal calques. Retain familiar technical loanwords only when they are standard and clearer to users. (descărcare, not „download”; link, not „link de descărcare” when the context already makes that clear)
- Keep terminology and capitalization consistent across the interface, especially for repeated actions and status names. (Use „Reluare” for the same resume action everywhere.; Use „Zonă de notificare” consistently.)
