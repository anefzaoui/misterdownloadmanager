- Phrase commands as direct imperatives. Use the verb first; keep button text short. (Begin aflaai; Voeg skakel by)
- Use the same imperative form for menu commands. Use a noun phrase only when the menu item names a view or category. (Lêer > Aflaai byvoeg; Gereedskap > Instellings)
- Phrase check boxes as concise descriptions of the option, not as commands to the user. Omit terminal punctuation. (Begin aflaai wanneer die toepassing begin)
- Address the user as jy/jou in explanatory text. Keep the tone friendly and direct; avoid the formal u and unnecessary politeness formulas. (Jy kan hierdie aflaai later hervat.)
- Ask confirmation questions directly, using a question mark. Name the action rather than adding a vague lead-in such as “Are you sure?”. (Wil jy hierdie aflaai verwyder?)
- Use concise noun phrases for labels and column headings. Include articles or prepositions only when Afrikaans grammar or clarity requires them. (Lêernaam; Aflaaigrootte)
- Use a short action phrase for an in-progress status, and a clear noun-and-verb phrase when showing a count. Use an ellipsis only for an action that is still in progress. (Kontroleer...; {n} aflaaie laai af)
- Keep error text grammatical even when the subject is understood from context. Use nie on both sides of the negated verb phrase. (Kon nie die lêer oopmaak nie: {error})
- Write event log entries as brief, readable statements or event labels. Keep the event first and add the item name after a colon when useful. (Aflaai voltooi: {name}; Aflaai het begin: {name})
- Use sentence case for titles, headings, buttons and labels; do not capitalize every word. Capitalize proper names and the first word of a sentence. (Aflaaibestuurderinstellings; Microsoft Windows)
- Write days of the week and months in lowercase, including in dates. (maandag 8 april)
- Use standard Afrikaans punctuation. Put no space before a comma, full stop, colon, semicolon or question mark; use one space after it. (Aflaai voltooi: {name}.)
- Use double quotation marks around quoted text, with no spaces just inside the quotation marks. Keep punctuation outside the closing mark unless it belongs to the quoted text. (Die status is “Voltooi”.; Kies “Hervat”. )
- Make agreement follow the implied or stated Afrikaans subject, not the English source. If a fragment omits its subject, keep the wording natural as a fragment. (Die aflaai is voltooi.; Kon nie oopmaak nie.)
- Use natural Afrikaans word order: place the finite verb in its usual main-clause position and keep separable verb particles at the end. (Laai die lêer af.; Jy kan die aflaai hervat.)
- Prefer established Afrikaans technical terms. Retain familiar loanwords or product names where they are standard; do not invent a literal translation just to avoid a loanword. (skakel; Windows)
- Keep terminology consistent throughout the interface, especially for the same action in buttons, menus, statuses and help text. (Gebruik “Hervat” vir die aksie om ’n onderbreekte aflaai voort te sit.)
- Preserve placeholders, variables and their order. Do not add spaces inside a placeholder or alter its spelling. (Kon nie {file} oopmaak nie: {error})
